\begin{abstract}
\noindent\singlespacing
We develop a two-period small open economy New Keynesian model to compare explicit
forward guidance with opacity when the central bank's inflation aversion is private
information. Under opacity, uncertainty about the central bank's reaction function
generates exchange rate misalignment and suboptimal period-1 allocations. Forward
guidance---modelled as a non-binding rate forecast---resolves this uncertainty,
improving welfare through two channels: an exchange rate channel specific to the
open-economy setting, and an expectation coordination channel. The welfare gain is
strictly positive when cost-push shocks are present and type uncertainty is genuine,
and is non-monotone in the degree of openness.
\end{abstract}
