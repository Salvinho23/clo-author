## Key Findings from Literature

1. **Theoretical consensus:** Forward guidance is theoretically welfare-improving (Woodford, Eggertsson-Woodford), but less powerful than the benchmark DSGE model implies (Del Negro et al., McKay-Nakamura-Steinsson, Angeletos-Lian).

2. **Empirical consensus:** Rate paths are moderately predictable and partially credible, but the marginal contribution of numerical paths over verbal guidance may be small (Andersson-Hofmann for RBNZ).

3. **Over-commitment costs are real but under-quantified:** The Riksbank experience (2010–2014) and the ECB (2022) show that published paths can generate costly over-commitment, especially when the bank pursues secondary objectives. No systematic cross-country regression exists.

4. **Methodological state-of-the-art:** Bauer-Swanson (2023) endogeneity correction and Jarociński-Karadi (2020) Delphic/Odyssean decomposition are now required for credible empirical work on forward guidance shocks.

5. **Active reform debate:** Bernanke (2024) recommends scenario-based communication for the BoE; Fed officials are discussing similar reforms. This is an active policy frontier with no empirical evaluation yet.
